# Designing a community repair intake

This original design proposal concerns a neighborhood repair workshop. It is a
conceptual process, not a report of measured workshop outcomes. The reader is a
volunteer deciding who needs which information before accepting an item.

## Intake workflow

Intake sends the item description to a safety reviewer and a parts reviewer.
The safety reviewer returns a safety note. The parts reviewer returns a parts
note. Both notes reach the coordinator. The coordinator returns an incomplete
request to intake for clarification. An accepted request can then be scheduled.
These arrows describe proposed information handoffs, not elapsed time or proof
that a repair is safe.

## Why the two reviews stay separate

A missing component and an unsafe device are different reasons to pause. Keep
their notes separate so a coordinator can see what remains unresolved. A fork
and join expresses that both reviews are needed. A loop expresses clarification,
not guaranteed eventual acceptance. The topology is an original process design.

## Reference transfer and its limits

The recovered agent-workflow corpus includes an orchestrator-workers figure.
That metadata is useful for finding a delegation reference. This design transfers
the question of how separate work rejoins; it does not claim to reproduce the
source figure's geometry, palette or typography. Human volunteers retain their
own judgment. Model automation is not evidence for workshop safety.

## Scope boundary

Scheduling, repair procedures and safety criteria are outside this proposal.
Do not add invented success rates or timelines. A brief written boundary is
more useful here than a decorative chart.
