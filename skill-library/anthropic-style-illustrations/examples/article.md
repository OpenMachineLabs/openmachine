# A small workflow for reviewing archive requests

An archive team needs a review process that keeps a request attached to its evidence. A short workflow helps reviewers hand off work without treating an incomplete request as a rejected one. The process below describes responsibilities, not measured speed or reliability.

## Receive the request

The coordinator records the requested collection and the research question. The record retains the requester's wording so later reviewers can distinguish the original question from their interpretation. Once the request is recorded, the coordinator passes the record to the archivist.

## Check the evidence

The archivist checks the record against the collection catalogue. Missing collection details are returned to the coordinator for clarification; the archivist does not invent a likely identifier. After the catalogue check is complete, the archivist sends the annotated record to the access reviewer.

## Review access

The access reviewer checks the annotated record against the collection's access conditions. A restriction is recorded alongside its reason, and an unresolved condition remains unresolved. After the access review, the reviewer passes the decision and its supporting notes to the coordinator.

## Return a traceable response

The coordinator returns the decision with its supporting notes. The response separates what the archive can provide from what still needs clarification. Keeping those notes attached lets a later reviewer revisit the decision without reconstructing the entire conversation.
