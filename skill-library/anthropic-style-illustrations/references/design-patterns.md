# Design patterns and boundaries

These patterns interpret the attributed observations in [the index](source-index.json).
Case IDs resolve to article and image links there. They are evidence for design
choices, not permission to copy a source image or a claim of fresh pixel review.

1. **Decision and exit — A2.** Name the intermediate artifact and show both the
   passing path and the negative exit. Do not turn unrelated benefits into a
   chain or add a decorative gate without a criterion.
2. **Alternatives versus parallel work — A3, A4.** Label whether branches are
   exclusive or simultaneous; give reconciliation its own named operation.
   Equal-sized cards do not establish equal effort, probability, or reliability.
3. **Feedback and termination — A6.** Distinguish the proposal from actionable
   feedback and expose an accepted exit. Independent sampling is not critique;
   iteration alone is not evidence of quality improvement.
4. **Overview and detailed exchanges — B1, B2, BL1.** Keep user-facing exchange,
   persistence, and provenance visible in an overview; move dense message timing
   to a separate detail view. Diagram spacing never measures elapsed time.
5. **Controlled conceptual comparison — C1.** Hold shared components stable
   while showing what changes and which information is actually selected.
   More resources do not establish better outcomes.
6. **Abstraction levels — C2, J1.** Use the same task to compare levels of
   detail, revealing the selected scope before optional expansion. A conceptual
   scale must not masquerade as a measured performance axis.
7. **Traceable transformation — D2, P2.** Preserve case identity and emphasize
   the specific operation that changes an artifact. Generated context is not a
   verbatim quote; an action menu is not an ordered workflow.
8. **Matched quantitative comparisons — D3, F4.** Preserve conditions, units,
   and direction of improvement; paired gaps can warrant connected points.
   Relative percentage and percentage-point change are different claims. This
   renderer supports simple bars/lines, not automatic dumbbell conversion.
9. **Composition and denominator — F6.** Keep the shared denominator and verify
   group/segment totals before choosing a stacked design. The current renderer
   does not implement stacked bars; do not silently substitute grouped totals.
10. **Comparison with actionable information — E5, E6, E8.** Hold query and
    result identity fixed and expose the next useful repair action. E8 is a
    contrast, never a target. Fewer characters or a serialization format alone
    cannot establish usability.
11. **Policy matrix — G3.** Use rows and columns for comparable attributes and
    preserve qualifications as source-anchored cell text or linked notes. Blank
    cells must not imply permission, equivalence, or missing policy conclusions.
12. **Controlled panels — H2, Q1.** Separate changed conditions from trajectories
    and repeat labels so color is not the only cue. Position and color acquire
    numerical meaning only when the source establishes it; this renderer does
    not implement scientific geometry or small-multiple chart grids.
13. **Hierarchy versus chronology — V2.** Use containment for scope and separate
    definitions, attempts, assessments, and measurements. A nested item does not
    establish the order of execution; do not force it into a flow diagram.
14. **Convergence and divergence — T1.** Show shared processing across surface
    variants when the article supports it. Check terminology against evidence;
    authoritative figures can still contain inconsistent wording.

15. **Preparation, iteration and evaluation boundaries — X2, MCP1.** Show
    one-time research and briefing apart from repeated work. Draw held-out,
    independent evaluation as a trust boundary and label the feedback loop.
    Do not imply the producer can see private evaluator data.
16. **Prove more than one aspect of success — X1, X7.** When the argument
    involves optimization, independent audits, scale transfer and baselines,
    use separate clearly titled panels or an ordered dot plot with its
    uncertainty row. Never combine different denominators or make elapsed
    time look like quality.
17. **Intervention next to measurement — X3, X6, X8.** Place matched
    conditions and their measured effects together. Reuse axes, condition
    colors and precise metric names across controlled small multiples.
    Dense token labels and specialist activation plots need custom layouts and
    actual viewing-size checks, not an automatic generic chart.
18. **Qualitative control and matched interface evidence — X4, X5, X9.**
    Keep the baseline scenario beside the changed condition, preserving the
    reader's ability to inspect the example. Match timestamps and viewports;
    a selected screenshot is neither a representative latency distribution nor
    a substitute for broader outcome evidence.
19. **Provenance before interpretation — SKY1, SWAP1, X2.** Separate source
    records, algorithmic transformations, learned judgments and independent
    checkpoints where they matter to the claim. A provenance path cannot be
    inferred from arrows alone: label who produced and verified each artifact.

These are **reusable information structures**, not CSS recipes or rights to
reproduce official artwork. `source-index.json` contains the full attributed
observations, inferences and transfer limits. Other editorial families (network,
dot plot, waterfall, error bars and scientific small multiples) may require a
bespoke original SVG or charting tool rather than the six generic renderer modes.

For every adaptation, record linked case IDs in the spec and exact article
anchors for the new content. Source cases guide structure; the new article
owns all substantive claims. Final semantic and visual judgment stays with the host.
